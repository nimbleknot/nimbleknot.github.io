Privacy Policy for Logic Trio

Last updated: 4 October 2026

Logic Trio is made by Nimbleknot. This policy explains, in plain language, what information the game handles and who receives it.

The short version

Logic Trio does not ask you for anything. There is no account, no sign-in, no name, no email address, no profile and no password. You can install the game, play it for years and never type a single personal detail into it, because there is nowhere in the game to type one. (An optional feedback form opens in your browser; see Contact.)

Nimbleknot does not run a server and does not keep a database, and the game never sends Nimbleknot your name, your email address or your precise location. (If you write to Nimbleknot yourself, your message carries your email address; see Contact.)

Your puzzle progress stays on your phone.

What the game does contain is advertising, analytics and crash reporting from Google, and those Google libraries collect some technical information automatically. That is what the rest of this policy is about.

What stays on your phone

All of this is stored on your device only, and Nimbleknot cannot read it:

  • which puzzles you have solved, your best times and your stars
  • your streak, your freezes, your gold days and your monthly trophies
  • how many hints you have
  • your settings: theme, language, sound, vibration, colour-blind patterns, how you place a crown, and your reminder time
  • your daily reminders, which are scheduled and shown entirely by your phone
  • a small diagnostic log of what happened in the game — which puzzle was opened, which hint was used — kept on the phone so a problem can be described if you ever report one, and cleared with everything else below

If you have Android's automatic backup switched on, your phone may copy this save file to your own Google Drive, the same way it backs up your other apps. That backup belongs to you and sits in your Google account. Nimbleknot has no access to it. You can turn it off in your phone's own settings.

You can erase all of it at any time: Me → Settings → Delete my data. That removes the save completely and the game becomes new again. Uninstalling the app also removes it. Since version 1.0.2, the same button also clears the Analytics records still waiting on the phone to be sent and gives the game a new random app-instance ID, so what the game sends afterwards is not joined to what it sent before. Records already sent are deleted on the schedule under How long it is kept. The button does not change your advertising ID or the random installation ID used for crash reports and the game's settings, and it does not switch Analytics off: where you were asked, your consent answer decides that.

What Google's libraries collect automatically

Logic Trio uses four Google libraries that collect information. Each one sends what it collects to Google's servers; Nimbleknot has no server of its own. What Nimbleknot can see of it afterwards, in its own Google accounts, is set out under Where the information goes. Three more Google libraries are in the app and are described at the end of this section: the billing library, which has nothing to sell, the review library, which is the only part of the whole game you can type into, and the update library, which asks Google Play whether a newer version of the game is out.

Google AdMob (advertising)

Logic Trio is free and is paid for by ads. To show them, Google's advertising library collects and shares:

  • your IP address, which gives an approximate location such as a city or region — never a precise one
  • your interactions with ads — that an ad was shown, closed or tapped
  • diagnostic information about how the ad performed
  • your Android advertising ID and App Set ID — resettable identifiers that belong to your device, not to you personally

Google uses these to choose which ads to show, to measure them, and to detect fraud. All of it is encrypted while it travels.

Google Analytics for Firebase (understanding how the game is played)

This tells Nimbleknot how the game is used, so it can be improved. It collects an app-instance ID, your advertising ID, an approximate location from a masked IP address, and records of what happened in the game — which puzzle you opened, how long it took, whether you used a hint, which ad you were shown.

These records describe the game, not you. Logic Trio deliberately never sets a user ID, and where it summarises a player it uses ranges — "between three and six solved", not the exact number — or a simple setting such as which theme is chosen, so that no summary can be traced back to a single person.

Where you were asked to consent, this collection follows your answer — not just the advertising. If you refused, Logic Trio does three things rather than one: it tells Google you refused, it switches this collection off, and it throws away what is still waiting on the phone to be sent and resets the app-instance ID. What had already been sent before you refused cannot be called back by any app: Google Analytics deletes it after 14 months (see How long it is kept). Nothing further is recorded unless you change your answer at Me → Settings → Your data → Privacy choices, and saying yes later is the only thing that turns it back on — travelling to a country where the question is not asked will not undo a refusal. Where no law requires the question and you were never shown the form, this collection runs as described above. In the US states listed under If you live in the United States, opting out of selling and sharing keeps these records out of Google's advertising but does not switch them off.

Firebase Crashlytics (crash reporting)

When the game crashes, this sends Google a report containing the technical details of the crash, your device model and Android version, and a crash-installation identifier. Logic Trio attaches nothing else — no custom information, no identifiers of yours. This exists so that a crash affecting you can be found and fixed.

Firebase Remote Config (settings without an update)

This lets Nimbleknot adjust numbers in the game — how often an ad appears, how many hints something gives — without publishing a new version. To do that it sends your country, language, time zone, Android version and the app's own identifiers.

Google Play Billing (in the app, with nothing for sale)

The app also contains Google Play's billing library. A purchase was built and tested during development and then switched off before release, and the library stayed where it was.

When the game starts, it asks Google Play two questions: whether the store is reachable on this phone, and whether the Google account already signed in to the Play Store has bought anything from Logic Trio. The answer is always no, because Logic Trio has nothing for sale — no product exists to buy, and there is no payment screen anywhere in the game to reach. That exchange happens between your phone and Google Play, the store you are already signed in to. Nimbleknot receives nothing from it, no purchase is ever made, and so there is no purchase or payment information about you anywhere.

It is named here because it is genuinely inside the app, and anything inside the app belongs in this policy even when it sells nothing. If anything is ever offered for sale, this policy will be updated before that happens.

Google Play In-app Review (the one place in the game you can type)

After a good day the game may ask Google Play to show you its review sheet — Google's own panel, with a star rating and a box you can write in. It is worth being clear about this one, because it is the single exception to everything above: it is the only place inside Logic Trio where there is anything to type into. (The feedback form under Contact opens outside the game, in your browser.)

Google decides whether that sheet actually appears; the game only asks, and Google often declines. If it does appear, you are never obliged to use it, and closing it changes nothing in the game.

What you write there does not go to Nimbleknot — it goes to Google Play, and it is published publicly on the Logic Trio store page, under the name and picture of the Google account you use on the Play Store, exactly like any other Play Store review. That is how Play Store reviews work everywhere; it is mentioned here so that nobody is surprised by it.

Google Play In-app Updates (checking for a newer version)

Since version 1.0.3 the app also contains Google Play's in-app updates library. When the game starts, and each time you come back to it, it asks Google Play whether a newer version of Logic Trio is available. To answer, Google Play collects device metadata, the version of the game on your phone and the list of its installed modules and asset packs. Google says it uses these to work out whether an update is available and how large it would be, that the data is encrypted, that it is not passed to any third party, and that it is deleted after a fixed period.

That exchange happens between your phone and Google Play, the store the game was installed from. Nimbleknot receives nothing from it. If a newer version is waiting and your phone has not updated by itself, Home may show one line, An update is ready. A tap opens Google Play's own update sheet. You can decline there and play on. That the line was shown or tapped is recorded with the game's other Analytics records, described above.

What Logic Trio never collects

  • your name, email address, phone number or postal address
  • any password, payment detail, bank or card information
  • your precise location, or GPS of any kind
  • your contacts, calendar, photos, files, microphone or camera
  • anything you type into the game itself — there is nowhere in Logic Trio to type. The one exception is Google Play's own review sheet, described above: what you write there goes to Google and appears publicly on the store page, never to Nimbleknot. The optional feedback form under Contact is not part of the game: it opens in your browser, and what you choose to type there does reach Nimbleknot
  • purchases. Logic Trio 1.0 sells nothing. There is no in-app purchase in this version, so no purchase information exists to collect. If that ever changes, this policy will be updated before it does.
  • anything it knows comes from a child under 13. Logic Trio is not directed at children and is made for players aged 13 and over.

Your choices

  • Reset or delete your advertising ID. Android → Settings → Privacy → Ads. You can reset the ID, or delete it entirely, which stops ads being personalised to it.
  • Choose what ads you see, and whether the game measures you at all. If you live in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, where the law requires a choice, Logic Trio shows you Google's consent form the first time you play, and you decide there. You can change your answer later at Me → Settings → Your data → Privacy choices. That row appears only where the law provides it. Your answer is honoured by both halves of the app, not just the ads: refusing switches off the Analytics collection described above as well, and throws away what is still waiting on the phone to be sent; what was already sent is deleted by Google Analytics after 14 months. Refusing does not restrict the game in any way — you keep every puzzle, every feature and every hint, and simply see less relevant ads.
  • Opt out of the sale or sharing of your information, in the United States. In the twenty states listed under If you live in the United States, go to Me → Settings → Your data → Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information. What that does, and the other ways to opt out, are set out in that section.
  • Erase your game data: Me → Settings → Delete my data.
  • Ask about your data. Write to nimbleknot.games@gmail.com. The Analytics records and crash reports are filed under random IDs that the game never shows, not under a name or an email address, so a message cannot be matched to them; Delete your data says plainly what can and cannot be done. Questions are welcome and will be answered.

Where the information goes

The information described above goes to Google, which processes it as the advertising and analytics provider. Google's own handling of it is covered by the Google Privacy Policy and, for advertising, How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Advertising does not stop at Google, and you should know that plainly. AdMob sells the advertising space by auction, so when an ad slot is filled, a request describing the opportunity — your advertising ID, an approximate location from your IP address and your device type — is offered to the advertising companies bidding for it. Those companies are Google's advertising partners, there are a few hundred of them, and the list changes as Google adds and removes them. Nimbleknot does not choose them individually and receives nothing back from them.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, you can see every one of them by name and refuse them one at a time. On the consent form Logic Trio shows there, Manage options lists each partner with what it collects and how long it keeps it, and each has its own switch. You can also reopen that form at any time from Me → Settings → Your data → Privacy choices.

Selling and sharing, in the sense US law gives those words. Nimbleknot is paid for showing ads, not for handing over information about you, and it holds nothing from the game that names you. But the auction described above passes your advertising ID, an approximate location and your device type to advertising companies so that they can bid to show you an ad. The privacy laws of California and several other US states count that as "selling" or "sharing" personal information, and as "targeted advertising". Logic Trio therefore treats it as exactly that. If you live in the United States, below, sets out what is involved and gives you a way to opt out. What reaches Nimbleknot itself is the Analytics records and crash reports described above, in its own Google Analytics and Firebase accounts, filed under random IDs. Nothing described here tells Nimbleknot who you are.

How long it is kept

  • Your save, on your phone: until you delete it (Me → Settings → Delete my data) or uninstall the game. A copy in your own Google Drive backup follows your Google account's backup settings.
  • Google Analytics records: each record of what happened in the game is deleted after 14 months. Google clears out expired records once a month, so a record can outlast 14 months by a few weeks. The random app-instance ID they are filed under is cleared out the same way once your phone has sent nothing for 14 months. Totals built from them, such as how many players solved Monday's puzzle, describe no single player and are kept.
  • Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics): 90 days, after which Google starts deleting them from its live and backup systems.
  • Remote Config: Firebase keeps the random installation ID the game asks for its settings with until the developer deletes it, under Firebase's own terms. Once the game is uninstalled, nothing on your phone carries that ID any more.
  • Advertising (AdMob): Google keeps advertising data for the periods set out in How Google retains data we collect. The advertising partners in the auction keep what they receive for the periods each one states; in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Manage options on the consent form lists them partner by partner.

Nimbleknot keeps no other copy of any of it.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland

European data protection law (the GDPR, the UK GDPR and the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection) gives you some extra information and rights.

Who is responsible. The developer named on the game's Google Play listing, trading as Nimbleknot, decides what the game collects and why. Google provides the Analytics, crash-reporting and Remote Config services the developer uses. For advertising, Google also decides some uses of the data itself, under its own privacy policy. Where the developer lets Google use Analytics data to improve Google's own services, Google decides those uses itself as well.

Why each use is allowed:

  • Personalised ads and Google Analytics: your consent, given on Google's consent form. You can withdraw it at any time at Me → Settings → Your data → Privacy choices; withdrawing does not undo what was done before.
  • Ads that are not personalised, which is what you see if you refuse: legitimate interest. The ads are what pay for a free game.
  • Crash reports and Remote Config: legitimate interest, in finding and fixing crashes and adjusting the game without an update. These run whatever you answer on the form.

Information sent outside Europe. Google may process this information in the United States and in other countries. It relies on the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework and its UK Extension, the Swiss–U.S. Data Privacy Framework, and the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.

Your rights. You can ask for access to the information held about you, and for it to be corrected, deleted, restricted or given to you in a portable form, and you can object to the uses based on legitimate interest. The records are filed under random IDs rather than your name, so the developer can act on them only where they can be matched to you; Delete your data explains what that means in practice. Write to nimbleknot.games@gmail.com. You also have the right to complain to the data protection authority where you live or work: in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office; in Switzerland, the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner.

If you live in the United States

California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Florida, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maryland, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Oregon, Rhode Island, Tennessee, Texas, Utah and Virginia have consumer privacy laws. Whether each one reaches a developer of Nimbleknot's size differs from state to state, so Logic Trio gives players in all twenty the choice and the rights below.

Opting out of selling, sharing and targeted advertising. When Google's systems place you in one of those states, Me → Settings → Your data has a row called Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information. It opens Google's page My data preferences: choose Don't sell or share my data and tap Save and close. From then on:

  • Ads are no longer personalised to you. Google's advertising switches to what it calls restricted data processing. The ads are chosen from the game and your rough location, not from what you have done elsewhere, and the advertising companies in the auction receive requests marked the same way.
  • Logic Trio tells Google Analytics not to use your data for advertising, so the game's records are kept out of Google's advertising.
  • The game's own measurement and crash reports carry on. They are how Nimbleknot learns what to fix and improve, and they are neither sold nor used for ads.
  • Nothing in the game changes. Every puzzle, feature and hint stays exactly as it was.

Your choice is stored on your phone. You can open the row again at any time and change it back by choosing Allow the sale or sharing of my data. Delete my data does not remove it. Uninstalling the game, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, does, so opt out again after a reinstall.

Other ways to opt out, wherever you are. The row appears only where Google's systems detect one of those states. On any phone you can also delete your advertising ID (Android → Settings → Privacy → Ads). After that, ads in Logic Trio and in every other app can no longer be personalised to it.

Global Privacy Control. Logic Trio is an app, not a website, and Android does not pass the Global Privacy Control signal to apps. If Google's consent tool ever records one for your phone, Logic Trio treats it as an opt-out: it asks only for ads that are not personalised, and it keeps its Analytics records out of Google's advertising.

What is collected, and who receives it. Since the game was released, Logic Trio has collected the following, all of it automatically from your phone through the Google libraries described above:

  • Identifiers: your advertising ID and App Set ID; the random app-instance, crash-installation and installation IDs; and your IP address.
  • Internet and app activity: what happened in the game (which puzzle was opened, how long it took, whether a hint was used), which ads were shown, closed or tapped, and diagnostic information.
  • Approximate location: a city or region worked out from your IP address. Never GPS, never precise.
  • Device information: device model and type, Android version, language, country and time zone.

This is used to show ads and measure them, to understand how the game is played so that it can be improved, to find and fix crashes, and to adjust the game without an update. Google also uses the advertising data to detect fraud. How long each part is kept is set out under How long it is kept.

  • Sold or shared, for advertising: your advertising ID and App Set ID, your IP address and the approximate location worked out from it, your device information and your interactions with ads. These go to Google. The advertising ID, an approximate location and your device type also go through Google's auction to the advertising companies bidding in it, so that they can choose, show and measure ads.
  • Given to a service provider: the Analytics records, crash reports and Remote Config information. These go to Google, which provides those services to Nimbleknot. Until you opt out, Logic Trio also lets Google use the Analytics records for its advertising, which these laws may count as sharing. Opting out turns that off.

Logic Trio collects no name, contact details, payment or purchase information, precise location, or anything else the law treats as sensitive personal information. It does not use your information to make decisions about you that have legal or similarly significant effects.

Players under 16. Logic Trio does not ask your age, so Nimbleknot has no actual knowledge that it sells or shares the personal information of anyone under 16. The game is made for players aged 13 and over.

Your rights. Depending on your state, you can ask to know what personal information is held about you and to receive a copy of it, to have it corrected, and to have it deleted. You can opt out of its sale, sharing and use for targeted advertising, as described above. Nimbleknot will never treat you differently for using any of these rights: the game does not change.

How to ask. Write to nimbleknot.games@gmail.com with "US privacy request" as the subject. Someone you have authorised can write for you, and Nimbleknot may ask them to show your permission. You will get an answer within 45 days. If the law allows longer and more time is needed, you will be told so within those 45 days.

What can and cannot be done. The records are filed under random IDs that the game never shows, and nothing links them to a name or an email address. So an email cannot be matched to your records, Nimbleknot cannot verify that any record is yours, and it will not claim to have found or deleted something it could not find. What does work is set out on Delete your data: Delete my data in the game erases the game's own data on your phone, deleting your advertising ID cuts the link to it, and the opt-out above applies your choice about selling and sharing.

If the answer is no. If Nimbleknot turns down your request, you can appeal: reply to the answer with the word "appeal". The request will be looked at again and answered, with reasons, within 45 days. If you are still not satisfied, you can contact your state's Attorney General, and in California also the California Privacy Protection Agency.

Children

Logic Trio is made for players aged 13 and over and is not directed at children. The game is not enrolled in Google Play's Families programme, contains no content aimed at children, and is not tagged as being for users under the age of consent. If you believe a child under 13 has used the game and that information was collected, write to nimbleknot.games@gmail.com and it will be dealt with.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it and the new version appears at this same address. Changes that expand what is collected — such as switching on an in-app purchase — are made before the change reaches players, never after.
